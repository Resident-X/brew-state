# Controller boards: design constraints

**This file is local, like `reference-machine.md`.** It holds what the replacement controller boards for this machine are designed against, each constraint with where it came from. An adopter fitting a different machine, or the same machine on a different supply, replaces this file along with the declaration it is derived from.

**This is a derived view, and its sources govern.** Nothing here is declared for the first time. Every figure is read off, or worked out from, a source named beside it: the machine's declared configuration (`reference-machine.md`), the channel enumeration (`controller-io.md`), the component specification (`component-specification.md`), a working design document in `controller-design/`, a module's published datasheet, vendor page or schematic cited by its address and the date it was read, an owner statement with its date, a requirement criterion, or a decision in the graph. Where this file and one of those disagree, the source is right and this file is corrected (`DEC-MACHINE-RECORD-FOLDER-PER-MACHINE`). To change a supply fact, change `reference-machine.md` § Supply and re-derive the figures here from it, never the other way round.

What is not here: choosing track widths, wire gauges, clearances or parts to meet these figures. That is design work, done against this file.

## Requirement-derived figures

The figures this project's requirements impose on the boards, reduced to what a board designer works to. Each entry gives its value with units, its evidence grade, the source a reader holding only this repository can follow, how the value was derived, and the requirement criterion or record entry it comes from. A figure that cannot yet be derived is marked **pending** instead of carrying a value.

### Evidence grades

Each value carries one of these grades. They are not levelled: a figure is no stronger than the weakest grade among the inputs it was derived from, and a derived figure says which input set its grade.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **measured** | Read with an instrument on this machine, the reading recorded with its conditions. |
| **owner statement** | Stated by the machine's owner from direct knowledge, dated, and not yet confirmed by inspection or by a document. |
| **schematic** | Read off the service manual's circuit diagram. |
| **parts list** | Read off the service manual's parts list. |
| **published unverified** | From a published source outside this repository — a standard, a datasheet, a photograph of the same model — cited by its address, whose text has not been checked at that source for this entry. § Carried-forward module properties distinguishes two kinds of entry under this grade, read at source and relayed, and says what the grade means for each. |
| **inferred** | Worked out by reasoning or arithmetic from other entries, or estimated with no measurement behind it. The derivation is shown so the inference can be checked. |
| **unmeasured** | A quantity known to matter for which no value has been established. |
| **project assumption** | A value chosen where no fact exists, so that the design has something to work to. Every one is marked to replace: an adopter puts their own value in its place, and the project replaces it when the fact is established. |

A value that a decision in the graph fixes — a fuse rating, say — is a design choice rather than a fact about the machine. It carries no evidence grade; the entry names the decision instead.

**Pending** is a mark, not a grade. A pending entry names the work that will settle it, in words and by its node in the graph. The mark ends only when that work records the fact and its source. Any document derived from this file shows the mark, not a value.

Two pieces of work end most of the marks below, and each is required to record what it ends. A figure that a chosen part or a designed circuit fixes, such as a branch fuse's rating, a rail's budget, or the time a protective device takes to clear and the energy it lets through, is recorded by **the design task for the board that carries it**. Those tasks do not exist yet: the partition and feasibility task creates one for each board once the division between boards is chosen, and each records, with its source, every figure this file holds pending against the design of its board (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C5`). A figure of a bought module, such as the supply module's isolation rating, is recorded by **the partition and feasibility task's choice of form** for each function, which records each such figure held pending against it with its source, read from the module manufacturer's current datasheet (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C1`). That choice comes first and decides which parts there are to read figures from.

### Proof-of-life removal interval

The interval within which loss of the controller's proof of life must remove heater power. It is derived, not chosen, per `DEC-PROOF-OF-LIFE-INTERVAL-BOUNDED-BY-FIRST-PROTECTIVE-TRIP`. Each heater is taken fully on at the top of the declared span, starting from the highest temperature its casting reaches while the controller is holding it, across the plant model's declared corners, with the lower bound of its thermal mass. The interval is the time that heater takes to reach its retained protection's trip point, and the shorter of the two heaters sets it.

That starting temperature cannot yet be taken from a control that holds the casting, so the interval is pending.

**Finding: no interval can be derived from the casting as the control stands.** The generated peak record, `docs/casting-peak.md`, which `python3 firmware/emulation/tools/run_casting_peak.py` regenerates, shows that under the control as shipped the coffee casting reaches 156.8 °C, plus 2.0 °C of declared sensing error, 158.8 °C, against the 107 °C coffee-thermostat trip, and the steam casting 192.2 °C, plus 2.0 °C, 194.2 °C, in a 20-second wand draw (a longer draw can climb higher: the record's draw-length table holds 191.0 °C at 20, 60 and 120 seconds and reaches 216.1 °C at 300). A time to a trip point that starts from a casting already past it is not a figure, and the steam peak understates a longer draw, so no proof-of-life interval can be derived from them. The work that bounds the casting is `DEL-CASTING-MARGIN-HELD-IN-EVERY-PHASE`. Nothing in this finding is a board figure. The peaks as the record gives them, graded inferred from the plant model (the casting has not been measured):

| Coffee thermoblock | 156.8 | 2.0 | 158.8 | Warm-up with no draw, served at 100.0 °C, at corner 2 of the enumeration (`brew.thermal_mass_j_per_k` at its low end) |
| Steam thermoblock | 192.2 | 2.0 | 194.2 | A 20-second steam draw, with the ready temperature at 186.0 °C, at corner 15 of the enumeration (`steam.heater_power_w` at its high end) |

Each of these, and what the arithmetic leaves out, errs toward a shorter time: each trip point is the low end of its tolerance, the power is taken at the element's cold resistance, which its rising resistance only lowers (and the steam element's 47.0 Ω is itself a lower bound), the thermal mass is its lower bound, and the arithmetic ignores both the loss to the room and the heat capacity of the water held in each block. The starting temperature is the one input that does not yet err that way, which is why the interval waits for it.

**Figures from the commanded temperature, which the casting's peak will replace.** Until the peak is derived, the only starting temperatures the graph declares are the highest the controller commands. They are shown here with the times they give, so the derivation has something to be checked against. They are not the interval. Each is an upper limit on its heater's time, which bounds the interval only from the side the interlock cannot use: an interlock timed to one of these figures could act after the trip it exists to pre-empt.

| Commanded temperature | Coffee thermoblock | Steam thermoblock | Grade | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Highest temperature commanded | 100 °C | 138.9 °C | inferred | Coffee: a brew target is refused at or above the saturation temperature `CONTROL_SATURATION_C`, accounted for in `firmware/params/control.declaration` and valued at `firmware/src/control/control.c:71`, so 100 °C bounds every admitted target from above. Steam: the loop has two phases, both declared `@estimated` in `firmware/params/steam_control.declaration`. While nothing is drawn it commands `ready-temperature-c`, 125 °C. During a draw it commands pressure instead, held inside a band whose top is `draw-pressure-ceiling-bar`, 1400 milli-bar. Converted through the declared saturation relation in `firmware/params/thermoblock.params` (`steam.saturation_temperature_c` 100 °C and `steam.pressure_bar_per_k` 0.036 bar/K), that ceiling is the higher of the two: 100 °C + 1.4 bar / 0.036 bar/K = 100 + 38.9 = 138.9 °C |

The times from the commanded temperature:

- Coffee: 192 J/K × (107 − 100) K = 1344 J; 1344 J / 1215 W = 1.1 s.
- Steam: 216 J/K × (195 − 138.9) K = 216 × 56.1 = 12 120 J; 12 120 J / 1362 W = 8.9 s.

Why these do not bound the interval from below, as the interlock needs: in both loops the casting runs hotter than the commanded figure, by an amount no declared figure fixes. The coffee loop drives the temperature of the water leaving the block, not the block itself, and the two are not a fixed offset apart (`firmware/src/control/control.h`). During a draw the steam loop drives pressure, and the block stands hotter than its measured pressure implies through the saturation relation, by the pressure the draw has cost the path (`steam.pressure_fall_bar_per_ml`, `@estimated`, in `firmware/params/thermoblock.params`). A casting that starts hotter starts nearer its trip point and reaches it sooner, so each time above is longer than that heater's real time, by an amount not yet known: an upper limit, not the interval. The coffee side shows how much that matters: it has 7 K between its commanded ceiling and its trip point, and each kelvin the casting stands above 100 °C takes 192 J/K / 1215 W = 0.16 s from its 1.1 s. Which heater sets the interval is likewise left to the derivation, because the two castings' excesses over their commanded figures are not the same.

Once derived, a measured thermal mass, a different trip point, or a change to what the controller commands or to the plant model's corners re-derives the interval; the basis does not change.

## Carried-forward module properties

What each bought module in the earlier controller design supplied without being specified, stated as a constraint on whatever replaces it (`DEL-HW-DESIGN-CONSTRAINTS.C4`): the isolation it provided and what that isolation separated, its output behaviour, including its state while powering up, in reset, unpowered and with its control signal disconnected, the input thresholds it presented, and its default states. Ratings a module's datasheet specifies outside those four classes, such as a pressure sensor's range, overload and burst, are chosen with the part by the partition and feasibility task (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C1`) and are not carried forward here. Whether each function stays a bought module or becomes a circuit the project designs is not decided here. The partition and feasibility task decides it (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C1`), and every constraint below holds either way. A constraint binds the load or function it names, never a circuit: the replacing design may serve several modules' functions with one circuit, such as one zero-cross detector for both pumps or snubbing shared between channels, and the form each function takes, and what it shares, is decided by that same task.

**The earlier design** is the working design in `controller-design/` (`controller-design/README.md` § Where we are): its bill of materials `controller-design/control-system-bom.csv`, its orderable part numbers `controller-design/part-numbers.csv`, its module wiring `controller-design/module-interconnect.csv` and its decision log `controller-design/decisions.md`. Facts those documents already state are cited by file and row. Where an entry repeats a value so that the outcome beside it can be read, the value stands beside the source it is cited to, and that source governs, as it does for every figure in this file.

**None of its parts were bought.** Owner statement, 2026-10-13: none of the earlier design's parts were ever bought — not the modules, the heater switches, the sensors, nor anything else. They were candidates picked from AliExpress and other vendors' listings while the direction was a board of discrete modules, and the product photographs and listings the working design cites are vendors' listings, not parts in hand. No part is here to measure, so every property below is read from the module's datasheet, its vendor's page or its published schematic, relayed by a working design document that read one, or is marked as not established.

**What published unverified means here.** The grades are those of § Evidence grades. An entry graded **published unverified** is one of two kinds, and its grade says which:

- **read at source**: the entry cites a manufacturer's or vendor's document by its address, given in the module's introduction or in the entry, and that document was read on 2026-10-13. The grade means the figure is the manufacturer's published claim, read at its source but not established on a part.
- **relayed**: the entry cites a working design document in `controller-design/` that records a vendor's figure from a listing or datasheet this record could not read it in: the working document gives no address for that source, the address did not answer on 2026-10-13, or the vendor's pages that were read do not state the figure. The grade then has the meaning § Evidence grades gives it: the figure's text has not been checked at its source for this entry, and the working document cited is where a reader can follow it.

Where neither a source read nor a relayed figure gives a value, the entry grades the property **unmeasured**.

Each property ends in one of three ways:

- **Constraint** — what the replacing circuit must do, stated as an outcome a designer can check, with the requirement criterion or decision that makes it binding under *From*.
- **Set aside** — not carried forward, with the reason: no requirement or decision needs it, or the replacing circuit must not inherit it, in which case *From* names the criterion or decision inheriting it would breach.
- **Pending** — a constraint whose value no datasheet, schematic, requirement or decision yet fixes, naming the work that settles it. Each is listed in § Pending marks above.

### Nothing in the earlier design provided

**Removing heater power when the controller stops.** No module the earlier design specified provides it. *Held off undriven* covers a controller that is absent, not one that has hung: a hung controller can hold a drive input driven, and a heater switch's ordinary failure is a short. **Which passive parts are listed.** One rule, applied on every channel. A passive part is listed where the channel's function, or a retained device's characteristic, rests on it: the divider and filter that turn a thermistor into a reading, the pull-ups a dry contact or a pulse output wants, the flyback diode that protects a relay coil's driver, and any passive that is rated for the mains or bridges the barrier, such as a mains-side sense resistor, suppression across a mains load, and a designed converter's bulk and Y-class capacitors. Every other passive is left to the design task for the board that carries it (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C5`), which chooses its value: the series resistors on an opto-triac's LED and gate, base and gate resistors, the pull-downs that hold a drive off, decoupling and bulk capacitors on the logic rails, and the microcontroller's reset and boot parts. So is the programming and debug header. Those passives are of the classes the Basic 0603 resistor and capacitor rows below already price (C25804, C14663).

**Not here: the proof-of-life interlock.** The interlock that removes heater power when the controller stops is built into the first controller hardware, but how it works is design work inside that hardware, which `DEC-PROOF-OF-LIFE-INTERLOCK-IN-FIRST-HARDWARE` leaves open.
