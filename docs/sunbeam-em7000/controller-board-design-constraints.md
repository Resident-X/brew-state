# Controller boards: design constraints

**This file is local, like `reference-machine.md`.** It holds what the replacement controller boards for this machine are designed against, each constraint with where it came from. An adopter fitting a different machine, or the same machine on a different supply, replaces this file along with the declaration it is derived from.

**This is a derived view, and its sources govern.** Nothing here is declared for the first time. Every figure is read off, or worked out from, a source named beside it: the machine's declared configuration (`reference-machine.md`), the channel enumeration (`controller-io.md`), the component specification (`component-specification.md`), a working design document in `controller-design/`, a module's published datasheet, vendor page or schematic cited by its address and the date it was read, an owner statement with its date, a requirement criterion, or a decision in the graph. Where this file and one of those disagree, the source is right and this file is corrected (`DEC-MACHINE-RECORD-FOLDER-PER-MACHINE`). To change a supply fact, change `reference-machine.md` § Supply and re-derive the figures here from it, never the other way round.

What is not here: choosing track widths, wire gauges, clearances or parts to meet these figures. That is design work, done against this file.

## Requirement-derived figures

The figures this project's requirements impose on the boards, reduced to what a board designer works to. Each entry gives its value with units, its evidence grade, the source a reader holding only this repository can follow, how the value was derived, and the requirement criterion or record entry it comes from. A figure that cannot yet be derived is marked **pending** instead of carrying a value.

### Evidence grades

Each value carries one of these grades. They are not levelled: a figure is no stronger than the weakest grade among the inputs it was derived from, and a derived figure says which input set its grade.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **measured** | Read with an instrument on this machine, the reading recorded with its conditions. |
| **inspected** | Read off this machine by eye, without an instrument: a first-pin mark, a connector housing, the face a part is mounted on, a marking printed on a part or label, which terminal a wire reaches. Recorded with the date and the item that supports it. It is kept distinct from **measured**, which needs an instrument, and from **owner statement**, which is a recollection not made by inspecting the machine for the entry. |
| **owner statement** | Stated by the machine's owner from direct knowledge, dated, and not yet confirmed by inspection or by a document. |
| **schematic** | Read off the service manual's circuit diagram. |
| **parts list** | Read off the service manual's parts list. |
| **published unverified** | From a published source outside this repository — a standard, a datasheet, a photograph of the same model — cited by its address, whose text has not been checked at that source for this entry. § Carried-forward module properties distinguishes two kinds of entry under this grade, read at source and relayed, and says what the grade means for each. |
| **inferred** | Worked out by reasoning or arithmetic from other entries, or estimated with no measurement behind it. The derivation is shown so the inference can be checked. |
| **unmeasured** | A quantity known to matter for which no value has been established. |
| **project assumption** | A value chosen where no fact exists, so that the design has something to work to. Every one is marked to replace: an adopter puts their own value in its place, and the project replaces it when the fact is established. |

The grade **inspected** is added by the machine-as-found record (`SOL-HW-MACHINE-AS-FOUND-FIT-RECORD`), which uses it in `reference-machine.md` § Controller enclosure as found and in `connector-pinouts.md`. No row graded before it was regraded: `connector-pinouts.md` § Grades in this file and the shared vocabulary maps that file's own grades onto this table in one stated correspondence.

A value that a decision in the graph fixes — a fuse rating, say — is a design choice rather than a fact about the machine. It carries no evidence grade; the entry names the decision instead.

**Pending** is a mark, not a grade. A pending entry names the work that will settle it, in words and by its node in the graph. The mark ends only when that work records the fact and its source. Any document derived from this file shows the mark, not a value.

Two pieces of work end most of the marks below, and each is required to record what it ends. A figure that a chosen part or a designed circuit fixes, such as a branch fuse's rating, a rail's budget, or the time a protective device takes to clear and the energy it lets through, is recorded by **the design task for the board that carries it**. Those tasks do not exist yet: the partition and feasibility task creates one for each board once the division between boards is chosen, and each records, with its source, every figure this file holds pending against the design of its board (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C5`). The figures of a bought module, such as the supply module's isolation rating, are recorded by **the choice of form** for each function, which is made and records each such figure with its source, read from the manufacturer's current datasheet (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C1`; `controller-board-function-forms.md`). Its candidates prove a form feasible and are not selections: the part fitted is selected by the design task for the board or assembly that carries it (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C5`).

### Supply budget

*Finding: the clip is rated at the fuse's rating, not at the overload band the fuse permits.* The sustained draw is held to 10 A by the controller's arbitration (`REQ-POWER-BUDGET-001`), so the clip is inside its rating in service; the 15 A band is a fault current, up to half an hour, which the clip's sheet does not rate. No clip rated above 10 A in the 5 × 20 mm size was found in the sheets read. The fuse's rating is **kept at 10 A**: a larger fuse would raise the band to 18 A (1.5 × 12 A) over the same clip and the relay's 16 A. The clip's 15 A rating is **open**; the act that settles it is naming a 5 × 20 mm clip whose maker rates it for 15 A, or recording that the clip is accepted at 10 A for the fault band. Either is the main board's schematic leaf's to draw (`SOL-HW-MAIN-BOARD-PROVEN-AND-LAID-OUT`).

**What the judgement that the chain is rated for the fuse leaves open.** The chain is judged rated for the fuse's band only where a rating was read: the link copper at 1.005, the relay contact at 1.33 (1.07 on the VDE figure) and the neutral pour at 1.22. Three elements are not so judged: the clip, at 10 / 15 = 0.67 of the 15 A band (the sheet rates it at the fuse's rating, not above it), the 6.3 mm tab, whose current rating is not on the drawing read, and the shunt, which is not chosen. These stay open with the schematic leaf's criteria that carry them: `SOL-HW-MAIN-BOARD-PROVEN-AND-LAID-OUT.C10` (every pending figure recorded with its source and its mark ended, which includes the clip's, the tab's and the shunt's ratings and the fuse's let-through) and `.C12` (the drawn board carries the entry fuse and surge clamp as judged, and a part that departs from a judged figure is a recorded finding). The tab's current rating is settled by asking its maker, Dongguan Bozhong (the BTC-3-PCB634908T), for the blade's rated current, or by the bring-up temperature reading of the tab at 10 A; the shunt's by the schematic leaf naming a part whose sheet gives 0.25 W continuous or more.

*Finding: the entry fuse priced in the cost record is not slow-blow.* The part priced, `SCF520PF10A250V` (catalogue C22447845), is described in its maker's sheet (SETfuse SCF520(P), V1.0, 2024-2026, from the part's catalogue entry) as a fast-acting ceramic cartridge (快断): it opens in at most 30 minutes at 2.1 times its rating and at most 30 ms at 10 times, its melting I²t is 235 A²s and it breaks 1 500 A at 300 VAC. The decision asks for slow-blow, so that the machine's inrush and the 15 A band do not open it. The Multicomp Pro 5.2 × 20 mm time-lag family (Farnell sheet, V1.0, 12/08/19) has rows at 8 A and 12 A and none at 10 A. **The one slow-blow 10 A part in the cost record, read.** The cost record also lists `SCT520PT10A250V` (catalogue C22447834, 887 in stock, 0.1716 USD at the read price). Its maker's sheet was fetched from the part's catalogue entry and read on 2026-10-28 (SETfuse SCT520(P), V1.0, 2024-2026, `.stage0/datasheets/lcsc-c22447834`); it describes the series as slow-blow (慢断), to IEC 60127-7, UL 248-14 and GB/T 9364.7. The sheet gives its melting-time limits as a table (its curve is a graph marked *for reference only*, not read as a figure): at 2.1 × the rating the fuse opens in at most 30 minutes, at 2.75 × in 0.75 s to 80 s, at 4 × for the 4 A to 10 A ratings in 0.15 s to 5 s, and at 10 × in 10 ms to 150 ms; the average melting I²t of the 10 A rating is 500 A²s, taken at 10 × the rating; its breaking capacity is printed as 10 kA at 250 VAC (UL, 0.4 A to 10 A) and 2 kA at 250 VAC (CQC and TUV, the 10 A rating), 2 kA at 300 VAC for the 0.4 A to 8 A ratings, against the project's 6 kA: 10 / 6 = 1.67 on the UL figure and 6 / 2 = **3.0 times short** on the CQC and TUV figure. ### Interlock removal time and drive

The interlock is a relay in the live feed (`controller-board-division.md` § The interlock block), driven by a two-channel circuit that turns the controller's proof of life into coil current. This section holds the arithmetic that the chosen interval, 100 ms, is met by it, and what each controller rest state does to the drive. The circuit, its faults and the options it was chosen against are in `controller-board-division.md` § The interlock block, *The drive*. The figures from a maker's document carry the document, revision and the date it was read; a figure the design requires of a part not yet chosen is marked *required*, is not a reading, and is checked on the chosen part's own sheet where the part is drawn (`SOL-HW-MAIN-BOARD-PROVEN-AND-LAID-OUT`).

**The refresh the circuit assumes.** The firmware's control step is 10 ms (`CONTROL_STEP_INTERVAL_MS`, `firmware/src/control/control.h`). The drive is held to a rising edge at least every **12 ms**, the 10 ms step plus 2 ms of allowance for the step's own jitter, a figure chosen here; the firmware follows it and the built board shows it (`DEL-HW-BRING-UP.C9`).

**Timeout against the refresh.** The time the gate stays above the 1.8 V at which the FET still carries the coil (its on-resistance is specified there) must exceed the 12 ms refresh. The shortest case: pump output after one edge, with the diodes at their required drops, 4.65 V (rail 4.75 V less 0.1 V) − 0.35 − 0.75 = 3.55 V; the coupling capacitor at its least, 70 nF, against the reservoir plus gate at their most, 5.24 nF, transfers 70 / (70 + 5.24) = 0.930 of the difference each edge, so with the decay between edges the gate stands at 0.930 × 3.55 / (1 − 0.070 × e^(−12 / 23.6)) = 3.30 / 0.958 = 3.45 V just after an edge. The shortest time constant is 5.544 MΩ × 4.49 nF = 24.9 ms, less 5.3 % for the diode's 50 nA leakage across the 105 MΩ the 5.25 V and 50 nA make: 23.6 ms. The gate then stays above 1.8 V for 23.6 ms × ln(3.45 / 1.8) = 23.6 × 0.650 = **15.3 ms**. That is 3.3 ms (27 %) over the 12 ms refresh and 5.3 ms (53 %) over the 10 ms step. At 1.8 V the coil is still fed: two FETs at 3 Ω and 90 mA drop 0.54 V, leaving 4.75 − 0.54 = 4.21 V against the 3.50 V pick-up, 0.71 V over. **That is at the sheet's 23 °C:** its coil figures are initial values at 23 °C (the sheet's coil-data note), and this record assumes 70 °C enclosure air (§ Enclosure air temperature). The sheet gives no temperature coefficient. A copper coil's resistance rises 0.393 % for each kelvin, 47 K from 23 °C to 70 °C is 18.5 %, and the pick-up voltage, which is the same ampere-turns through a larger resistance, rises by about as much; both are taken at +20 % here, a stated worst case and not a reading. The coil at its top tolerance is then 68.2 × 1.20 = 81.8 Ω and the pick-up 3.50 × 1.20 = 4.20 V; the coil carries 4.75 / (81.8 + 6) = 54 mA and stands at 54 mA × 81.8 Ω = 4.43 V, **0.23 V (5 %) over the pick-up** and no longer 0.71 V. The margin is thin but positive; it reaches zero at about +27 % on both figures, and the 20 % is an assumption. The consequence for the drive supply: the 5 V rail's low end of 4.75 V is what holds the margin, so the logic supply's tolerance, read where the supply is drawn, must keep the rail at or above 4.75 V at the coil's feed, and a rail that sags under the coil's current at 70 °C removes the margin and the contact drops out in normal running. Bring-up records the coil voltage at pick-up and at the held level with the enclosure warm (`DEL-HW-BRING-UP.C9`). The drop-out voltage also rises with temperature, which shortens the removal time, so the 0.5 V used above errs long.

**The proof-of-life pulse the arithmetic needs.** The transfer through the coupling capacitor and into the reservoir is the 70 nF in series with 5.24 nF, 4.88 nF, through a total series resistance of the buffer's output and the pump diode. Taken at no more than 1 kΩ (the buffer's 0.1 V at 1 mA is 100 Ω, the diode's dynamic resistance the rest; *required*, not read), the time constant is 4.9 µs, and five of them, 24 µs, complete the transfer to under 1 %. The output must therefore stay high for at least **25 µs** for each rising edge to pump the figures above, and stay low for at least as long for the clamp diode to restore the coupling capacitor; the firmware's pulse, raised at the end of one control step and lowered at the start of the next, is milliseconds in each phase, three orders over, and the figure is stated so that a refresh built from a short pulse is checked against it.

|---|---|---|
| Running | A rising edge each 10 ms step | Both gates held; the contact is closed |
| Main loop stopped, timers and interrupts running | Held high or low by the last write, unless firmware toggles it from a timer or an interrupt: **the circuit cannot refuse that** (*What the circuit cannot tell*, below) | A level has no edge: each coupling capacitor passes nothing, both gates decay, the coil drops within 88.7 ms of the last edge. A toggle from a timer or an interrupt is a train of edges and holds the coil |
| Halted (debugger, sleep) | Holds its last level | As the row above |
| Reset | High impedance; the two pull-down resistors hold the buffer's input low | No edge; as above |
| Controller unpowered (3.3 V lost, 5 V present) | Low or floating, held low by the pull-downs | No edge; as above |
| Whole supply lost | The coil has no supply | The coil's own current decay and the release: 18.0 ms at most by the rows above, 8 ms of it the sheet's |
| Power-up | The buffer's input is held low as the rail rises | No edge until the firmware writes one |
| Stores | The coupling capacitor blocks a level and carries only an edge's charge; the reservoir's charge falls through its bleed in the 69.7 ms above; the coil's energy falls through the clamp in the 10 ms above; no latch, timer or memory element is in the circuit | None holds the coil above drop-out beyond the interval after the last edge. A single stray edge, such as the output's first write after reset, buys at most 69.7 ms |

**What the circuit cannot tell.** The drive accepts any rising edge of the required size and spacing, whatever produced it. A timer or interrupt that toggles the proof-of-life output while the main loop has stopped makes the same edges as a completed control step, and the circuit holds the coil on them. This is a **named residual**. Requirement `REQ-SAFETY-CHAIN-001.C2` says that what demonstrates the controller running "must not be satisfiable by a controller that has stopped making progress". The circuit meets it for the states in the table above in which the output is a level or absent: a stopped, held, reset or unpowered controller, a floating or driven-to-a-level output, and the stores. It does not meet it for the state in which timers or interrupts keep running after the main loop has stopped. **The only guard against that state is the firmware design, verified on the built boards by `DEL-HW-BRING-UP.C9`** (a main loop that has stopped while its timers and interrupts still run does not keep heater power available, and the refresh is at the period assumed here). **No hardware means is added**, because a circuit that tells a loop's progress from a timer's edge needs a second element that the loop must answer, for instance a challenge and a response word on a pair of pins with a checker, or a second timer in an independent part, which adds a device, its firmware half and its own failure modes to a drive already of 19 parts, and the least-parts principle of this drive weighs against it; the requirement is met in the firmware design and the bring-up check, and this record does not claim more than the circuit does. The output edge and the 12 ms refresh are also the firmware's to produce: the firmware under `firmware/src` holds no proof-of-life output yet, so the 10 ms step and the 2 ms allowance are the figures that output must meet, not a behaviour read from it.

**What remains outside this record.** The removal time on a built board and the ceiling the interval sits under are out of scope (`DEL-HW-BRING-UP.C9`, `SOL-PROOF-OF-LIFE-INTERVAL-DERIVED-FROM-HELD-CASTING-PEAK`). The firmware's refresh is held by its own work.

## Carried-forward module properties

What each bought module in the earlier controller design supplied without being specified, stated as a constraint on whatever replaces it (`DEL-HW-DESIGN-CONSTRAINTS.C4`): the isolation it provided and what that isolation separated, its output behaviour, including its state while powering up, in reset, unpowered and with its control signal disconnected, the input thresholds it presented, and its default states. Ratings a module's datasheet specifies outside those four classes, such as a pressure sensor's range, overload and burst, are chosen with the part (the candidates are in `controller-board-function-forms.md`) and are not carried forward here, except the steam-path transducer's, whose required proof and burst pressures and range bounds are stated in § Steam-path transducer pressure so that the part is chosen against them. Whether each function is a bought module or a circuit the project designs is decided in `controller-board-function-forms.md` (`DEL-HW-PARTITION-AND-FEASIBILITY.C1`), and every constraint below holds either way. A constraint binds the load or function it names, never a circuit: the replacing design may serve several modules' functions with one circuit, such as one zero-cross detector for both pumps or snubbing shared between channels, and the form each function takes, and what it shares, is decided in that record.

**The earlier design** is the working design in `controller-design/` (`controller-design/README.md` § Where we are): its bill of materials `controller-design/control-system-bom.csv`, its orderable part numbers `controller-design/part-numbers.csv`, its module wiring `controller-design/module-interconnect.csv` and its decision log `controller-design/decisions.md`. Facts those documents already state are cited by file and row. Where an entry repeats a value so that the outcome beside it can be read, the value stands beside the source it is cited to, and that source governs, as it does for every figure in this file.

**None of its parts were bought.** Owner statement, 2026-10-13: none of the earlier design's parts were ever bought — not the modules, the heater switches, the sensors, nor anything else. They were candidates picked from AliExpress and other vendors' listings while the direction was a board of discrete modules, and the product photographs and listings the working design cites are vendors' listings, not parts in hand. No part is here to measure, so every property below is read from the module's datasheet, its vendor's page or its published schematic, relayed by a working design document that read one, or is marked as not established.

**What published unverified means here.** The grades are those of § Evidence grades. An entry graded **published unverified** is one of two kinds, and its grade says which:

- **read at source**: the entry cites a manufacturer's or vendor's document by its address, given in the module's introduction or in the entry, and that document was read on 2026-10-13. The grade means the figure is the manufacturer's published claim, read at its source but not established on a part.
- **relayed**: the entry cites a working design document in `controller-design/` that records a vendor's figure from a listing or datasheet this record could not read it in: the working document gives no address for that source, the address did not answer on 2026-10-13, or the vendor's pages that were read do not state the figure. The grade then has the meaning § Evidence grades gives it: the figure's text has not been checked at its source for this entry, and the working document cited is where a reader can follow it.

Where neither a source read nor a relayed figure gives a value, the entry grades the property **unmeasured**.

Each property ends in one of three ways:

- **Constraint** — what the replacing circuit must do, stated as an outcome a designer can check, with the requirement criterion or decision that makes it binding under *From*.
- **Set aside** — not carried forward, with the reason: no requirement or decision needs it, or the replacing circuit must not inherit it, in which case *From* names the criterion or decision inheriting it would breach.
- **Pending** — a constraint whose value no datasheet, schematic, requirement or decision yet fixes, naming the work that settles it. Each is listed in § Pending marks above.

**Which passive parts are listed.** One rule, applied on every channel. A passive part is listed where the channel's function, or a retained device's characteristic, rests on it: the divider and filter that turn a thermistor into a reading, the pull-ups a dry contact or a pulse output wants, the flyback diode that protects a relay coil's driver, and any passive that is rated for the mains or bridges the barrier, such as a mains-side sense resistor, suppression across a mains load, and a designed converter's bulk and Y-class capacitors.
