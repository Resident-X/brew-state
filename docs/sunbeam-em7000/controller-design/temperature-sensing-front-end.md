## 1. Current position and why

- **One calibrated PT1000 water probe is the key sensor** (D9, owner decision for placement; fitting detail agreed). The controlled variable is the water (`REQ-BREW-TEMP-001.C2`), so the accuracy goes there: a PT1000 spike probe in the stream at the **coffee block outlet, before the 3-way valve** (H743 pin PA4). It sees brew and hot water, has the least transport delay, and leaves the downstream loss (valve, tubes, the hot bore) to a model.
- **The block NTCs (coffee, steam) and the wand disc NTC stay as casing sensors** (D10, chosen; no MAX31865). The casing matters for the protection margin against the thermostat trip and as an estimator state; about +/-1 degC is enough. Even a 2 degC casing error moves the arrival temperature by only about 0.1-0.4 degC through the bore heat exchange (estimate, unmeasured). Keeping them leaves `DEC-DEVICE-RETENTION-BOUNDARY` untouched.
- **Flat PT1000 chips are commissioning tools only** (D11, chosen): a temporary probe at the puck inlet (PA1), plus a spare. Nothing is fitted under the OEM boot clamp; no drilling into the blocks.
- **Why the casing accuracy target is about +/-1 degC.** The coffee block's commanded target sits below its 110 degC thermostat (parts list), and the thermostat's +/-3 degC tolerance means the worst-case margin is a few degrees. `reference-machine.md` (Protection) ties that margin to `REQ-MEASUREMENT-001.C1` and says absolute accuracy matters more than repeatability on this channel; `controller-io.md` puts the figure at +/-2 degC. So roughly +/-1 degC absolute is comfortable, and repeatability alone is not enough.
- **Accuracy versus speed is not ruled on anywhere in the graph (to confirm).** A thermoblock is low-mass and can move degrees per second, so sensor lag becomes loop phase and caps bandwidth. This is the "wrong axis" pattern if the distinction is drawn where the component sits rather than where the consequence lands. Flagged, not resolved. The step-response speed of the fitted NTC versus any probe has not been measured. **Method to settle it:** a step-response test on the bench, driving the block from cold and recording the NTC against a fast reference. If the fitted NTC is slow (because the mounting, not the bead, dominates), the speed argument for keeping it falls away and the choice rests on mechanical risk and retention boundary alone, which are probably still enough but would change the reasoning.

**Why not (rejected options):**
- *MAX31865 boards* (PT1000 migration front end): not needed. The PT1000 channels are read directly on the H743 16-bit ADC through a ratiometric divider, and the NTCs are not migrating.
- *Swapping the block NTCs for PT1000*: rejected; mechanical risk and `DEC-DEVICE-RETENTION-BOUNDARY`, and the casing needs only about +/-1 degC.
- *PT1000 chips under the OEM boot clamp*: rejected; chips are commissioning tools only.

## 2. The sensors

### Water PT1000 probe
- Stainless spike probe, about 3 mm x 30 mm, PTFE/FEP leads rated 150 degC or more. **Probe rating is 125 degC or more, 150 degC preferred** (not 200 degC). Must be PT1000, not PT100. The Adafruit PT1000 board, if ever used as a reference, is **3648**; 3328 is the PT100 version.
- Class A is claimed on the listing and **unverified**. Class A at 95 degC is +/-0.34 degC (IEC 60751). **Verify any marketplace "Class A" claim yourself:** about 1090 ohm at room temperature, ice and boiling point on the whole chain, heat-cycle and re-check.
- Calibrate it on the real divider-and-ADC chain at the ice and boiling points (boiling pressure-corrected).
- Lead resistance does not block 2-wire use: two-point calibration solves gain and offset together (the delta between two known temperatures gives the gain, so lead resistance cancels; either absolute point then gives the lead offset). Copper's +0.393 %/degC means a 0.2 ohm lead swinging 50 degC moves about 0.04 ohm, about 0.01 degC at PT1000 sensitivity.

### Probe fitting
A standard **tee used as an elbow**, straight at the block outlet: the outlet feeds one run port, the branch carries on to the 3-way valve, and the probe sits in the opposite run port through a compression gland (rated 15 bar and 125 degC or more; the probe must not be able to push out under pressure), **pointing back into the oncoming flow, tip facing upstream**, sheath along the flow axis (about 20 mm of a 30 mm probe immersed). The tee mixes the stream, gives the probe a long immersion and keeps its tip out of the sheath's wake; its only dead volume is the gap behind the branch. A Y with an upstream-facing angled probe port is the second choice. A plain T with the probe across the flow gives a few millimetres of immersion, a stagnant ring and a large stem-conduction error. The fitting is insulated from the casing (silicone sleeving or high-temperature foam on the stub and fitting); a thin layer on a small tube helps little (critical-radius effect: 10 mm of foam on a 6 mm tube improves the loss by only about 10%), so keep the stub short. This is general instrumentation practice, not checked on this block; the outlet port's thread and the tee's thread are unmeasured. A stream heated in a laminar channel is cooler at its core than its mixed average, so the reading is compared with a mixed temperature in test 7.

### Steam-output monitor (PA6)
A permanent third PT1000 channel (D23, owner decision, 2026-10-14) so a temperature monitor can be added at the steam output without borrowing the commissioning channel PA1, which stays a spare. **Channel only:** the probe, its rating, and where it sits at the steam output are not yet specified. Its temperature range is the steam side (the steam block trips at 200 degC), not the brew-line 125 degC figure, so the probe must be rated for that when chosen. Same 4.7 kOhm low-TCR divider, filtering and calibration route as the other PT1000 channels. ADC channel (ADC12_INP3 expected) not yet verified against DS12110. No control use is claimed for it.

### NTCs
- Coffee block (EM70025), steam block (EM70020) and wand disc (EM70024), three channels (PC0 / PC1 / PC4). **100 kOhm at 25 degC** (schematic RT1-RT3; 10 kOhm 1% dividers on the OEM board). **B is not established.** The wand disc's relation to the quantity it stands for is itself unestablished (`controller-io.md`), a separate problem from the block sensors.
- At 95 degC the NTC is of the order of 5-8 kOhm if B is about 3950 (assumed). With the **4.7 kOhm divider** at 3.3 V that is about 0.3 mA and 0.5 mW in the sensor (estimate), so self-heating is negligible.
- **Mounting (inferred from photos, to confirm).** The OEM NTC is a silicone-boot plug whose element sits in a slot on its flat face, pressed onto a flat plate of the block by a screwed clamp bracket with a compound ring; there is no wetted pocket, so mechanical risk of keeping it is low. Lifting a bracket confirms it.
- Calibrated in place against the water probe **under steady low flow after a short flush**, never at rest: stagnant water at the probe cools toward ambient and does not equal the casing. Fit three points to Steinhart-Hart rather than two points to a beta model (an R25/beta fit leaves real residual error away from the points; the three-coefficient form is stated to hold to about 0.01 degC across a wide span). **Bracket the operating region** (for example 90-105 degC rather than 0 and 100 degC out of habit). The estimator residual shows later drift.
- NTCs drift (beta ages with thermal cycling), so recalibration is a maintenance activity; no criterion requires it yet (section 8).
- Refit with non-conductive high-temperature thermal compound (paste, rated at or above block temperature) if an NTC is removed.

### Commissioning chips
Two flat PT1000 thin-film chips, Class A claimed (unverified), welded leads, rated 250 degC or more: one is the temporary probe at the puck inlet (PA1, ADC channel not yet verified against DS12110), the other a spare. Same verification as the probe. Check the spare on the tube next to the steam pressure sensor (section 5).

## 3. The read chain

- **Ratiometric divider.** Each channel is a **4.7 kOhm low-TCR (25 ppm/degC or better) metal-film** resistor from VREF+/VDDA (3.3 V) to the sensor, read on the H743 16-bit ADC from the same node as the reference, so supply variation cancels and reference accuracy leaves the error budget. For a PT1000 this is about 0.54 mA and 0.4 mW self-heating (a 1 kOhm reference would push about 1.4 mA and 2.6 mW). Six channels: 3 PT1000 (outlet probe PA4, puck-inlet chip PA1, steam-output monitor PA6) and 3 NTC. The board has no analog conditioning.
- **Specify by TCR, not tolerance.** Calibration absorbs initial tolerance (it solves for gain) but not drift. A generic 1% resistor pack is usually 50-100 ppm/degC and does not meet this. This supersedes the earlier recommendation of 0.1% parts.
- **Filtering.** 100 nF X7R at each ADC pin; with about 1 kOhm source impedance that rolls off near 1.6 kHz, far above a thermal signal sampled at 100 Hz. `REQ-ELECTRICAL-INSTALL-001.C8` requires precision sensing to hold accuracy while the kilowatt loads switch.
- **Averaging over whole mains cycles**, synchronised to the zero-cross capture, with a median filter against dimmer spikes. A ferrite bead and 100 uF at the 5 V input are optional, only if the bench shows the supply's 65 kHz switching ripple on the sensing.
- **Noise target.** The 16-bit front end aims at about 0.05 to 0.2 degC noise (estimate, unmeasured; test 5 measures it).
- **Heater ripple on the casing reading (estimate).** At about 1.5-2 K/s of no-flow heating and 0.5 s on (50 % duty, 1 s window): 1.5-2 K/s x 0.5 s = 0.75-1.0 K worst case at the casing, before sensor lag and heat spreading smooth it (reviewer put it at about 0.4 to 1.0 K; the lower end is not reproduced from the stated numbers). The earlier 0.9-1.9 K figure is withdrawn. Block mass and heating rate are unmeasured.
- **16-bit buys resolution, not accuracy.** The error stack is dominated by sensor tolerance and beta uncertainty, which no ADC upgrade touches.
- **Calibration** per section 2; the water probe is the reference for the NTCs (test 2).

## 4. Group and bore hardware, and loss estimates

**What the hardware turned out to be.** The brass group outlet is built into the centre of the thermoblock, and the brew water reaches it through a tube that runs down the block's open central bore, ending in a brass nut that is very hot to touch. The group metal is thermally tied to the block: the casing temperature is, to a good approximation, the group metal temperature, and no separate group sensor is needed (there is also no exposed brass to mount one on). The tube and nut in the bore exchange heat with the casing: rough numbers, a metal tube there picks up about 15% of the gap between its water temperature and the casing's at shot flow (1.2 ml/s) and about half at pre-infusion flow (0.3 ml/s); a PTFE tube picks up much less. The portafilter and basket, which leave the machine and cool in air, are the one state nothing observes.

**Block ports.** Inlet and outlet at opposite ends of the coffee block [WEB, low confidence; to confirm on the machine].

**Expected loss from the block outlet to the group** (rough, unmeasured): a few degC through the valve and tube at shot flow, partly offset by bore reheating, with a larger and more uncertain figure at low flow. The brew branch after the valve is partly copper (it warms by conduction) and about 15 cm of PTFE (about 1.9 ml of water). Shorten and reduce the bore rather than relying on foam. The honest answer is to measure it (tests 3 and 4).

## 5. Pressure taps

- **Brew tap (D13, agreed).** Cold side, **between the new brew OPV and the block**, a direct tee with no capillary or leg: the block, valve and tube drop is only hundredths of a bar at espresso flow (estimate), the pump's peaks (what opens the OPV) are visible there, and the OPV setting can be checked in place against a blocked outlet. The base machine's brew pump has a damper and **no OPV** (owner statement; a forum post agrees, low confidence; to confirm on the machine), so the tap sees the damped pressure and what the damper hides is settled in commissioning. A tap after the block would also work with the same drop leg (the water is liquid at about 95 degC) and read the pressure the puck sees, but the gain is only the block, valve and tube drop (unmeasured), it adds thermal mass and dead volume to the controlled-temperature path, and it loses the OPV-node check. The second block outlet (to the vent; a dead end while the vent is closed in a shot) is the cheap place to test it. The controller opens the vent at the end of the brew, so a tap there would read the depressurisation too. The cold-side tap also sees the vent depressurise, because the check valve opens forward as downstream pressure falls.
- **Steam tap (D14, owner decision).** A normal hairpin-clip-style tee on the **steam side between the steam block and the manual steam knob valve**, with a plain PTFE drop leg (not a capillary; matched to the OEM tube size and wall; measure OD and ID) about **100-150 mm** long running downhill to the sensor at the lowest point, no rising section, so the leg fills with condensate and air rises back into the line. It sits upstream of the valve so a closed valve stops milk suck-back reaching it, and away from the block so the water seal does not boil off. **Cold-side steam tap is rejected (D22):** the cold-side reading adds a flow-dependent two-phase block drop that is unmeasured, and the saturation-temperature and fault checks need the pressure in the block. Rough, unmeasured: heat penetrates the stagnant column only mm to about 1 cm in minutes; check with the spare chip on the tube next to the sensor (aim under about 60 degC).
- **PTFE rating caveat.** PTFE is accepted only if its rating at the hot-side maximum is confirmed. The OEM steam line is already PTFE; match its tube size and joint method. If the rating cannot be confirmed, the capillary is only the fallback: a stainless capillary (about 3 mm OD x 1.5 mm ID, 300 mm) or 4 mm metal tube. The hairpin-clip temperature rating at steam is unconfirmed.
- **OPV and check-valve conditional (steam side).** The steam OPV is directly on the steam pump, downstream of the pump's own check valve (owner statement), opening at about 4-5 bar gauge and discharging to the drip tray. It caps the block pressure **only if no check valve lies between it and the block (to be inspected, F7)**; otherwise the pressure after the block is the saturation pressure at the block temperature, about 15.5 bar absolute at the 200 degC thermostat trip (about 17 bar absolute at the thermostat tolerance).
- **Hot-side rating rule (stated once).** Until the check valve question is settled, hot-side steam parts are rated for the 200 degC thermostat trip: about 15.5 bar absolute, about 17 bar absolute at the thermostat tolerance. If inspection shows the OPV does cap the block, saturated steam at 4-5 bar gauge is about 152-159 degC, so the rating drops to at least 160 degC and 8-10 bar gauge. The 0.5 MPa sensor's overload is 150 % FS = 7.5 bar gauge and its burst is 15 bar gauge, which the uncapped case reaches only at the thermostat tolerance (about 14.5 bar gauge at the trip, about 16 bar gauge at tolerance), and its overload is exceeded well before, so the sensor is not the pressure guarantee. The brew-line probe rating (125 degC or more, 150 preferred) is separate.
- **Sensors.** Two XDB401 3.3 V I2C units (D12): 1.2 MPa brew, 0.5 MPa steam, G1/4 BSP, on two separate I2C buses (I2C1 PB8/PB9, I2C2 PB10/PB11), both at the fixed 0x7F address, no multiplexer. The datasheet (owner-supplied, generic XDB401 family) gives -40 to 105 degC operating and -20 to 80 degC compensated, +/-1% FS, response time 4 ms. A brew tap after the block would sit at about 95 degC, outside the compensated range, with the 110 degC thermostat trip above the sensor's limit, so it would need a leg or capillary too. Confirm the 3.3 V variant's output rate and thread with the seller.
- **NSA2302.** The I2C chip is probably the NSA2302 (inferred): single-shot conversions, output rate set by OSR, address programmable in EEPROM. The achievable sample rate for ripple capture is to be bench-measured. Machine-side thread sizes are not yet measured.

## 6. Commissioning tests

Each uses the temporary puck-inlet probe, in a dummy puck or the basket path, read during steady flow after a flush unless stated.
1. Ice-point and boiling-point calibration of the water probe on the real chain.
2. NTC calibration against the water probe under steady low flow.
3. Cold-plug dip: after several minutes idle, start a shot and log the first five seconds at the puck inlet. A short flush or a plug-flow state is added only if a meaningful dip is measured.
4. Identification runs: cold start, second shot, and a shot after a hot-water draw, at several flows, to fit the downstream loss, the soak of the valve and tube, and the bore heat exchange (`REQ-STATE-ESTIMATION-001.C6`). A permanent second water probe is added only if the model's residuals demand it.
5. A noise check with the loads switching, and the pump pressure-ripple capture.
6. Dry-fill: with an empty or blind basket and a fixed pump drive, log the suction-side flow meter and the brew pressure tap from the pump start. The flow integrated to the first pressure rise is the empty volume ahead of the restriction (block, tubes, group); repeat after a long idle and straight after a shot to see how much the idle drain empties the block and how repeatable the fill is. Then fit the empty-basket resistance as a baseline, so the extra resistance with a puck can be read as the puck's. One pressure sensor sees only the total series resistance. Needs the flow meter's pulses per litre calibrated, the sensor's low-pressure noise floor and the I2C sample rate measured; none is known yet.
7. Mixed-cup check: at a steady low flow, collect a shot into a lidded insulated cup and compare its mixed temperature, with the cup's own heat capacity allowed for, against the probe reading; repeat at two flows. A consistent offset is calibrated out; one that varies with flow says the probe is reading the core and the fitting needs a stronger mixing feature upstream of it.

## 7. Pre-shot conditioning

A short hot-water flush conditions the probe, its stub and the common chamber of the 3-way valve and can run with the portafilter attached; run it at a low flow through the pump dimmer so the water is at temperature (about 1 kW cannot heat a fully open flow). It does not condition the brew branch, the bore or the portafilter; a group flush needs an empty portafilter locked in. The controller defers a target it cannot yet reach (`REQ-BREW-TEMP-001.C3`) and acts on the thermal state it is actually in (`.C5`).
