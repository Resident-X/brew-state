# Controller I/O enumeration

**This file is local, like `reference-machine.md`.** It answers one question: what does a controller have to provide to serve every channel this machine already senses or drives, plus every channel the graph has since decided to add? It is written by hand; `DEL-COMPONENT-SPECIFICATION.C6` (regenerating a specification and purchase list from the graph) is a later criterion of the parent task and is not attempted here.

**Refreshed 2026-10-12 against the controller design.** This version brings the enumeration current with the design recorded in this folder: the WeAct MiniSTM32H743 controller (`decisions.md` D1), solid-state heaters driven through a ULN2003A (D2), RBdimmer Standard pump dimmers (D3), a mechanical relay module for the two valves with the 2-way vent normally open (D5, D6), the outlet water PT1000 and two commissioning PT1000 chips alongside the retained NTCs (D9-D11), two XDB401 pressure channels, one of them on the brew line (D12, D13), two BL0942 heater meters (D17), and the operator-panel and gauge channel allocation (D18, D19). Where this file and `decisions.md` disagree, `decisions.md` wins; where the machine as found is described, the source is `reference-machine.md` and `connector-pinouts.md`, which this file does not edit. Status words follow `decisions.md`: Chosen, Owner decision, Agreed, Recommended, Owner statement, Parts list, Schematic, Published unverified, Inferred, Unmeasured. Anything the inputs do not settle is marked *to confirm*.

Every row traces to one of two sources: the machine's declared configuration in [`reference-machine.md`](../reference-machine.md) — sensing and actuation recorded there as already fitted — or a decision that added or changed a channel, named against that decision rather than appearing without provenance. A channel is not in this enumeration unless one of those two things put it there; a channel a requirement wants but nothing has yet fitted or decided to fit is called out as open, not invented into a row.

What accuracy, resolution and update rate each channel needs was out of scope when `SOL-CONTROLLER-CHANNEL-ENUMERATION` first wrote these tables, because the work those figures had to be derived against — sensing error carried in the commanded margin — had not landed. It has since, and `SOL-CHANNEL-MEASUREMENT-FIGURES` adds the three columns to both tables below. The original slice kept part choice, cost, wiring and pin assignment out of scope; this refresh records the parts and pins the design has since chosen (the *Realised by* summary below and the Peripheral class cells) but does not reopen them, and the figures in the three columns are still read off declarations, never off a part. Still out of scope: the cost of anything, and the machine's plumbing, which `DEC-PLUMBING-LEFT-AS-BUILT` already leaves as the factory built it — with the exception that the pressure-path parts (over-pressure valves, dampers, taps) are listed as BOM items whether or not the OEM fits them (D15), and are tabulated under *Protective devices and pressure-path parts* below.

Several rows below were first written against a declaration that had not been checked against the machine's own service manual. That document settles more than was assumed: the three temperature sensors' element type, the two solenoid coils' voltage class, and the interface the two gauges are actually driven through, each of which stood as *not established* here until `SOL-CHANNEL-ENUMERATION-CORRECTED` read the primary source. The same reading found three channels this enumeration never carried at all — a mains zero-cross detector, the operator panel's pushbuttons, and its indicator LEDs — which are added below on the same footing as every other row, because `reference-machine.md` now declares them. The operator-panel channels reach the main board over a harness rather than sitting on a board of their own: `DEC-U8-BOARD-RETENTION-UNVERIFIED` once left open whether a replacement could keep a panel board instead of driving those channels itself, and records that the question does not arise: the panel board is retained, but it carries no controller to speak to. They are enumerated here as channels a controller must serve directly, which is now the only way they can be served.

Five rows were brought current with a decision that changed what they demand, and `SOL-CONTROLLER-AND-MAINS-PARTS-DOWN-SELECT` updated them so a candidate is assessed against a live list rather than a stale one. With actuation settled as a per-half-cycle energise-or-idle decision below the hardware seam (`DEC-MAINS-PHASED-ACTUATION-BELOW-SEAM`), the heaters need a device that can be commanded without a mechanical relay's on/off-only limit; the pumps keep a phase-capable arrangement, which leaves only the granularity of modulation to measurement; and the reference needs both crossings of each cycle, which a half-wave circuit does not give. **Since this refresh, the zero-cross reference comes from the pump dimmer's Z-C output rather than from a purpose-built front end, and the heaters are zero-cross SSRs; `DEC-MAINS-PHASED-ACTUATION-BELOW-SEAM` has not been amended to match and a revision is owed (`h743-pin-allocation.csv`).** Those rows state their rate and resolution in supply half-cycles rather than in milliseconds, because the quantity belongs to the supply the machine is plugged into rather than to this design: the same rows read correctly on a 60 Hz supply, where a half-cycle is 8.33 ms and 1.2 of them fall in each 10 ms control step. Nothing depends on that count being a whole number — the residual carried forward between half-cycles is what makes the fractional case behave as the integer one does, and it is not an optimisation to be tidied away on a 50 Hz machine where the count happens to come out at one.

## What the control needs of each channel

The three added columns carry no figure invented for this document. Each is read off something this
design has already declared and committed to, and each cell names which one it took and which
requirement criterion that figure serves. There are four admissible sources and no others:

- **The delivery bands**, `firmware/params/tolerance.declaration`. `brew-temperature-band` (1000
  milli-c, i.e. ±1 °C, `@document`) is how far water reaching the coffee may sit from what was
  commanded; `flow-departure-band` (200 milli-ml-s, i.e. ±0.2 mL/s, `@estimated`) is the smallest
  departure a delivery is judged against.
- **The declared sensing error**, accounted for in `firmware/params/control.declaration` and valued
  in the control sources that file accounts for. That declaration deliberately carries no numbers —
  each figure has one definition, in the source, and one account, in the declaration — so both sites
  are named here: `CONTROL_SENSING_ERROR_C` on the brew channel (value `2.0f` at
  `firmware/src/control/control.c:114`) and `STEAM_CONTROL_SENSING_ERROR_C` on the steam channel
  (value `2.0f` at `firmware/src/control/steam_control.c:63`), both `@estimated` at 2 °C — the
  accuracy class a block-mounted domestic sensor ordinarily carries — and each already the figure
  its own loop's commanded margin is widened by.
- **The states and bands the steam path is held at**,
  `firmware/params/steam_control.declaration`. `draw-pressure-floor-bar` (1000 milli-bar,
  `@estimated`) and `draw-pressure-ceiling-bar` (1400 milli-bar, `@estimated`) are the two edges of
  the pressure band a draw is held inside; `ready-pressure-bar` (900 milli-bar, `@estimated`) is the
  threshold the feed pump is withheld below, and `ready-temperature-c` (125000 milli-c,
  `@estimated`) the block temperature the loop holds while nothing is drawn. **OPEN DECISION: steam heater with the knob shut:** the firmware figure `ready-temperature-c` (125 C) is a block temperature the loop holds while nothing is drawn, which is the OEM behaviour (`decisions.md` F10, as superseded by the owner statement of 2026-10-15: the OEM holds the block hot with the knob shut), and the trapped-pressure case (an S8 interlock on the steam heater, a hot-side relief valve, or both) is open in `open-items.md`; heating against a closed knob is not chosen or ruled out here.
- **The control loop's step interval**, `CONTROL_STEP_INTERVAL_MS` in
  `firmware/src/control/control.h` (10 ms, i.e. 100 Hz), accounted for as `step-interval-ms`
  `@estimated` in `firmware/params/cadence.declaration`. Every update rate below is stated as that
  interval or as a stated whole multiple of it; the full account of the interval is in *Compute and
  operator-interface demand*, further down.

**Declared figures that predate the outlet probe.** The sensing-error declarations above (`CONTROL_SENSING_ERROR_C`, `STEAM_CONTROL_SENSING_ERROR_C`) were written for the block-mounted NTC as the control sensor. D9 makes a calibrated PT1000 in the water at the coffee block outlet the key temperature sensor and demotes the block NTCs to casing sensors (D10). Whether those declarations are re-valued for the outlet probe is not decided in the inputs read for this refresh; the cells below carry the declared figures unchanged and say so (*to confirm*).

What a part costs is not among them. The parent task, `DEL-COMPONENT-SPECIFICATION`, applies cost to
a specification already settled rather than letting it shape one, and
`SOL-CHANNEL-MEASUREMENT-FIGURES.C2` carries that ordering into these columns by making a figure
whose named basis is a price inadmissible. So a figure adopted because a part happened to offer it
at a price is the case this ordering exists to exclude.

Every cell carries the marking its source carries: where a declaration marks its own value
`@estimated`, so does the cell, because a figure copied across without that marking would read as
established, which is exactly the failure those declarations exist to prevent. Where no admissible
declared figure reaches a channel, the cell says so rather than carrying a plausible number, and
where the quantity does not apply to the channel at all — the accuracy of a two-state contact
closure — the cell says that instead. This is the discipline the Signal and Peripheral class
columns already apply, extended to three more columns. Naming, against each cell recorded as not
yet derivable, the work that will settle it is `DEL-COMPONENT-SPECIFICATION.C3` and is not
attempted here.

No row claims a multiple of the step interval greater than one. A slower sub-cadence for a channel that plainly does not need reading a hundred times a second would be a figure this design has not declared anywhere, and inventing one is the thing the ordering above rules out; so every channel is provisioned at one control step, which is the budget the compute section below already reasons against.

Seven channel groups are not provisioned that way, and each says so in its own cell. Mains zero-cross detection is paced by the supply, which produces crossings whether or not a control step has come round — not a figure invented here either, but the mains frequency `reference-machine.md` § Supply already declares, and the exception exists because the quantity is an external event rate rather than a rate this design chose. The two heater mains-presence confirms are paced by the interval a command's effect is judged over, which the confirmation requirement sets rather than this document. The two BL0942 heater meters are paced by the module's own free-running UART packet (23 bytes, about 48 ms at 4800 bps, longer than a mains cycle; `module-interconnect.csv`) — slow integrated scalars, new with this refresh. And the low-water indicator and the buzzer are paced by the operator rather than by the loop: each has to reach somebody before they act on a machine that is not ready, which no declared figure of this design fixes and which this document does not invent one for.

## What the retrofit keeps

Each row below carries a **retention** state, because what a controller has to accommodate is not only which channels exist but which of the devices behind them survive the retrofit. `DEC-DEVICE-RETENTION-BOUNDARY` settles that boundary.

The distinction that makes the column useful is between three layers, not two. A **device element** — a switch contact, an indicator element, a motor winding, a thermistor bead, a heating element, a solenoid coil — is retained, and imposes a *characteristic* on whatever drives or reads it. The **conditioning network** around it — the resistors on the keys and the dividers on the thermistors — sits on the main board being replaced and is therefore a design choice; in this design those choices are now made (4.7 kΩ low-TCR dividers to the ADC reference for every temperature channel, the H743's internal pull-ups plus 47 Ω at the pin for the keys and switches, an external pull-down at every actuator input that must idle off). The indicators' series resistors and the gauges' stepper driver are the exception: those sit on the front assemblies and stay with them, so they are inherited rather than chosen. The **carrier board** itself is replaced outright: the controller is the H743 module plugged into female headers on a hand-built perfboard, with the mains modules (SSRs, dimmers, relay module, inlet filter and supply) off that board.

Reading those layers as one is what produced an earlier version of this section, which listed the button pull-ups as a retained constraint. They are not retained. The 2026-10-12 reading of sheet 3-3 (`connector-pinouts.md`) shows a 1 kΩ in series between each key line and its switch to ground, a 47 Ω at the MCU pin and no pull-up drawn, so the OEM appears to rely on the MCU's own pull-ups; this disagrees with the earlier reading of the 1 kΩ as pull-ups and is to be checked with a meter (*to confirm*). What a retained key imposes is that it is a dry contact to ground on a line shared with the indicator cathodes, not any particular resistance. The indicators' series resistors are the opposite case and were mis-sorted with them: those are on the panel and stay, so they are inherited.

The two front assemblies are not alike — `reference-machine.md` records both Holtek parts on the main control board, with the button and indicator panel carrying the indicator elements, the five keys and the buzzer, and the gauge assembly carrying the two movements, the stepper driver and the low-water indicator. Only the first is passive, so a statement about "the front panel" is true of one assembly and false of the other. So for the button panel's channels the element and the main board's circuitry separate cleanly. For the gauge assembly and the indicator series resistors they do not: those circuits are on retained assemblies and are inherited. The OEM panel, gauge and flow-meter harnesses are reused (D18).

This is also the record `OBL-PHYSICAL-CONFIGURATION-001.C1` and `.C3` ask for — what depends on a component, and which parts are reused and therefore owe a condition check before anything relies on them. `.C3` bites hardest not on any channel here but on the protective devices tabulated further down, which are all retained, none characterised, and none reachable by a controller that could notice. The record lives in this document rather than in the graph because allocation from a requirement to a physical component is not wireable in this engine, so the enumeration is the mechanism rather than a convenience copy of one.

## Compute and operator-interface demand

**Control law and estimator.** The estimator reconstructs the states the control law needs from whatever the sensing channels above provide, produces a running comparison between predicted and observed measurements, and delivers output at the rate the control law consumes it (`DEL-SIM-ESTIMATOR`) — recurring, real-time arithmetic on every control tick, not a one-shot cost. This competes with the sensing and actuation channels above for the same core and the same tick budget. The chosen controller is the WeAct MiniSTM32H743 (STM32H743VIT6, LQFP100, 480 MHz, 2 MB flash and 1 MB RAM, three 16-bit ADCs; D1, Chosen), whose onboard DC-DC accepts 3.3-5.5 V at 1 A max; it carries a TFT, microSD, QSPI/SPI flash and a DVP port the design does not use.

The rate itself is a figure the control loop already commits to, not an invented one: the step interval is declared once, at `firmware/src/control/control.h`'s `CONTROL_STEP_INTERVAL_MS` (10 ms, i.e. 100 Hz), with its basis recorded in `firmware/params/cadence.declaration` as `@estimated` — chosen so the loop samples far faster than the brew path's thermal time constants, which are seconds rather than milliseconds. That file is explicit that the figure is an estimate, not a validated sufficiency claim: "whether ten milliseconds is sufficient for the disturbances this machine actually sees is a question for the control-law work rather than a claim made here." So every channel above, and the estimator's own arithmetic, is provisioned against a 100 Hz tick — each channel must be sampled, and each actuation command issued, inside that budget — while whether 10 ms is itself the right interval stays open. What stays genuinely out of scope here, per this criterion's own boundary, is measuring the control law's actual execution time on target hardware — that is on-target work under a later phase, and is a different question from the declared cadence cited above.

**Operator interface.** The machine is reached one of two ways, and this enumeration states what each spends without choosing between them:

- A colour panel driven from the controller directly, which spends the controller's own display drive and memory bandwidth.
- A separate display board reached over a serial link, which spends two pins (the serial interface) and pushes the panel's own cost onto a second part instead of the controller.

Neither option accounts for the operator surface the machine already has. The five pushbuttons, the indicator elements and the two gauge steppers are channels in their own right in the tables above, and on the machine as built they reach the main board over two nine-way harnesses (reused, D18). The button and indicator panel is passive; the gauge assembly is not, carrying the stepper driver and the low-water indicator. A third future was once costed here — speaking the two-wire link between the OEM parts for two pins and keeping the panel board — and it does not exist: `DEC-U8-BOARD-RETENTION-UNVERIFIED` records that both microcontrollers sit on the main board, so those two wires connect two chips this project replaces rather than two boards, and the panel board is kept, but there is no controller on it to speak to. Serving these channels directly is not the conservative option; it is the only one. The cost is seven pins for the button panel — five bidirectional lines (PE4-PE8) carrying both the keys and their indicators, a timer output gating the anode rail (PB5), and the buzzer (PB4) — and six for the gauge assembly: five gauge drive lines through the 74HCT541 (two step clocks, two direction lines and a driver reset) and the low-water indicator (PA0). Thirteen, not the twenty-plus a separately-driven reading gives, and a headroom argument built on this enumeration carries that figure. The three ground-switched inputs (PE9, PE15, PC6) are additional. The display question itself is undecided and this refresh does not settle it; the board's own TFT is unused.
