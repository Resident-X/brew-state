# Component specification and list

**This file is local, like `reference-machine.md` and `controller-io.md`.** It answers the question those two leave open: given every channel the machine needs, what property must each part have, which part has been selected, does the selected part meet the property and on what evidence, and what is still undecided or unmeasured.

It is a refresh of the earlier `component-specification.md`, rewritten against the decisions of 2026-10-12 (`decisions.md`) and the current design data (`control-system-bom.csv`, `part-numbers.csv`, `module-interconnect.csv`, `passive-and-drive-components.csv`, `h743-pin-allocation.csv`, `h743-header-pinout.csv`). `decisions.md` records that none of its decisions is in the graph yet; where this file leans on one it says so. `DEL-COMPONENT-SPECIFICATION.C6` — regenerating this document and its list from the graph — is still a later criterion of the parent task and is not attempted here.

Tags used for provenance: **[chosen]** and **[owner decision]**, **[agreed]**, **[recommended]** are the status words of `decisions.md`; **[owner statement]**, **[parts list]**, **[schematic]**, **[published, unverified]**, **[inferred]**, **[unmeasured]** are as defined there. **[BOM]**, **[pin allocation]**, **[interconnect]**, **[passives]** name the data file the statement is read from. **To confirm** marks a claim kept from an earlier text or a source that the current inputs cannot settle.

Three rules govern what may appear below.

**The required property is stated independently of the part.** Each switched load names what its device has to do, carry, dissipate and fail into. The selected part is then held against that property. This is deliberate: a part recovered from another machine, or a replacement bought later, can be held against the property, but not against someone else's part number.

**Every figure traces to a declaration.** Currents are derived from the measured element resistances in `reference-machine.md` § Heating and load at the supply span that document declares. Nothing here introduces a figure that section does not carry, and where a figure cannot be derived the row says so rather than estimating.

**The controller is now selected, and the selection is still an input to the graph, not a graph fact.** The earlier text of this file declined to select a controller because `DEC-PREHARDWARE-TARGET-FAMILY` and `DEC-HEATER-DRIVE-GATED-BY-SUPERVISION` left the choice open, to be answered against measured plant parameters. The WeAct MiniSTM32H743 is now chosen (D1, [chosen]; reason recorded as its 16-bit ADCs). The earlier assessment candidate, an STM32F407 board, and the OEM Hongfa relays were not used: the F407 was replaced for the H743's ADCs, and the relays cannot be commanded at the switching rate the heater scheme needs (half-cycle or whole-cycle: open decision). Whether `DEC-PREHARDWARE-TARGET-FAMILY` (which the earlier text cited as nominating the F407 for the pre-hardware target build) has been revisited for the H743 is **to confirm**; `module-interconnect.csv` separately records a graph revision owed against `DEC-MAINS-PHASED-ACTUATION-BELOW-SEAM` (see the supply-timing section).

## What the supply is, and what changes off it

Everything below is stated against a declared supply rather than against this machine's. The reference build runs on a declared span of 216–253 V at 50 Hz, from a 10 A outlet behind an upstream breaker declared as up to 20 A (`reference-machine.md` § Supply, which grades each). Two things follow for an adopter elsewhere, and both are properties of their supply rather than of this design:

- **Frequency sets the switching cadence, not the control period.** Heater devices are specified as commandable once per supply half-cycle (OPEN DECISION: heater switching cadence (whole-cycle versus half-cycle): `h743-pin-allocation.csv` toggles at whole-cycle granularity). That is 100 commands per second at 50 Hz and 120 at 60 Hz; the device requirement is identical either way, and `DEC-MAINS-PHASED-ACTUATION-BELOW-SEAM` carries the residual forward so a non-integer number of half-cycles per control step behaves exactly as an integer number does.
- **Voltage sets the current, and the current sets the device.** These elements are fixed resistances, so halving the supply halves the current and quarters the power: the same two elements on 120 V would draw about 2.3 A and 2.6 A and deliver roughly a quarter of their rated heat, which is a different machine rather than the same one on a different plug. An adopter building to the same *heating* on a 120 V supply needs 120 V-rated elements instead, and `reference-machine.md` § What depends on these values records where that lands: the same installed heating at 120 V is over 16 A, so concurrent brew and steam is unavailable there regardless. Either way every current and dissipation figure below is recomputed from the adopter's own supply and element resistances; none of them is inherited.

This machine's 50 Hz is the supply's nominal, taken from the standard governing that supply rather than from a component's marking, as `REQ-MACHINE-CONFIGURATION-001.C9` requires (`reference-machine.md` § Supply, which grades it). Nothing below depends on its exact value: whatever depends on the supply's timing reads it live while the machine runs. The 50 Hz figure also appears in the dimmer acceptance test (about 100 zero-cross edges per second), which therefore measures the supply as well as the module.

## What each switched load needs of its device

Six mains loads, each specified by property. Currents are computed from the measured cold resistances in `reference-machine.md` § Heating and load at three points of the declared 216–253 V span: 230 V nominal, 240 V, and 253 V, the span's top, which sets every current and dissipation a device is sized for (its 216 V bottom sets the least heat delivered and no device rating); a cold resistance gives the higher current if these elements behave as metal heating elements ordinarily do, resistance rising with temperature. **That coefficient is assumed, not declared** — no document here gives one for either element. It is called out because the whole direction of the sizing case turns on it: were either element to fall in resistance when hot, the worst case would be the hot reading rather than the cold one, and every figure in this table would be a floor rather than a ceiling.

### Isolation, which every row above shares

`OBL-ELECTRICAL-THERMAL-SAFETY-001.C6` applies to all six loads and is stated once here rather than repeated per row, because the requirement does not vary between them: the barrier between the mains-referenced switching device and everything the controller, its sensing and the operator can reach must hold under fault as well as in service, and under the water, steam and repeated thermal cycling the machine actually runs in rather than under bench conditions.

For every one of the six, that means the device is commanded across an isolating barrier and never by a direct electrical connection to a controller pin. On the two pump channels the dimmer module's optocoupler (MOC3021) is that barrier and the requirement is a property of the module, and the vendor page states no isolation figure (read 2026-10-13), so it must be measured before it is relied on. On the two valve channels the relay module's opto input and the relay's own coil-to-contact separation provide it. On the two heater channels the barrier is inside the SSR, and the controller's pin reaches it only through the ULN2003A at 5 V; the SSR's own isolation figure is **to confirm** from the EARU datasheet. The heater channels are the consequential ones: they carry the largest current in the machine and they are the channels moved from a relay to a semiconductor.

Two further isolation items are new with the selected parts. The BL0942 energy meters (D17) are inherently mains-referenced, with a shunt in the current path and a resistor divider off mains, and **may not be connected at all until the module's isolation has been measured** (resistance between every header pin and every mains terminal reads open; treat as live until proven) [interconnect, pin allocation]. And the IRM-10-5 supply is rated 4.2 kVac isolation [BOM].

Creepage and clearance on the boards carrying these devices are a layout question this document does not settle, and no declared figure of this design bounds them. The BOM states 4–5 mm gaps on the hand-cut IRM carrier, that conformal coating does not replace creepage, and that the module's own 38.5 mm pin separation must stand on its own. The jurisdiction whose wiring rules apply is AS/NZS 3000 (`reference-machine.md` § Supply), which is a local assumption an adopter replaces along with the voltage and the frequency.

### The regression this specification makes visible

Meeting the half-cycle requirement (cadence is an open decision) on the two heater channels meant replacing a mechanical relay with a semiconductor device, and that is a regression in failure mode, not only a change of part. A relay's dominant failure is an open contact — not its only one, since contacts switching this current do weld — whereas a shorted junction is a semiconductor switch's ordinary way of dying rather than its unlucky one. That the selected SSR shares this failure tendency is **inferred** from its being a semiconductor output switch; it is not stated in any input and is to confirm on the EARU datasheet. On a heater channel the difference is the whole of `OBL-ELECTRICAL-THERMAL-SAFETY-001.C2`: a shorted output holds the element energised with no command and no way for the controller to intervene, which is precisely the state that criterion exists to forbid.

This is recorded here rather than solved here. The answer is not a better SSR — it is a series element that removes power independently of the switching device, which is what `REQ-SAFETY-CHAIN-001.C2` and `.C3` already require of the proof-of-life interlock. **Overtaken by `DEC-PROOF-OF-LIFE-INTERLOCK-IN-FIRST-HARDWARE` (2026-10-18): the interlock is built into the first controller hardware, not deferred.** The interlock is still not built: the master relay (Songle SLA-05VDC 30A, with aux contacts, which "cannot be the trip decider" because firmware may not be in the protective path) and the watchdog module (Jaycar/Freetronics XC4267) are BOM rows marked DEFERRED, and a candidate latching relay (CZH-LABS D-1022) is noted in `module-interconnect.csv`, deferred per `DEC-HEATER-DRIVE-GATED-BY-SUPERVISION`, which permits bench operation under human supervision without it and gates only the assembled unattended machine. Whether the graph now has covering work for the interlock is **to confirm**; the earlier text said it had none. The coupling remains: choosing the device the actuation scheme demands worsens the failure mode it protects against.

What the selected design adds is limited. The ULN2003A low-side drive with 10 k pull-downs holds both SSRs off while the controller is in reset or hung, which addresses a controller failure, not an SSR failing shorted. The BL0942 current reading (D17, [chosen], no BOM row yet) could in principle show heater current flowing with no command [inferred]; it detects, it does not remove power, and it is blocked on the isolation measurement above.

Both elements sit downstream of a per-block series thermostat (coffee block 110 °C, steam block 200 °C [parts list]) and, on the steam block, a thermal fuse (240 °C; rating [published, unverified]), neither of which the controller can reach (`DEC-HEATER-DRIVE-GATED-BY-SUPERVISION`). Those bound the hazard; they do not satisfy the criterion, which is about the controller's own failure rather than the temperature's.

**Simultaneity holds on the allocation as written, with the unverified rows named.** No channel is double-assigned among the 38, and the one overlap in the file (PC6) is on an optional TX. The six unverified items are the PA1, PA4 and PA6 ADC channels, the PA10 and PB5 timer alternate functions, and PA9's timer channel (not required: plain GPIO is assigned). Until those six are checked against DS12110 the allocation is a plan, not a proven placement.

**One row of the earlier assessment is resolved by a part.** The earlier text found that a 3.3 V controller output could not be guaranteed to clear the gauge driver's 3.15 V input high, and offered level shifters or a bench measurement of VOH. The 74HCT541 buffer on the perfboard is that level shifter (one octal part, socketed). It is also a cost that belongs to **retention** of the gauge driver rather than to the gauges, which sits with `DEC-DEVICE-RETENTION-BOUNDARY`.

**A requirement disagreement to settle.** The earlier enumeration said the two gauge step clocks need timer channels because an uneven step rate reads as needle stutter. The allocation file assigns all five gauge lines (PD0, PD1, PD4, PD5, PD6) as plain GPIO and does not say whether the step clocks are timer-driven. Whether the stutter requirement is met by a software-timed clock or needs timer outputs on two of those pins is **to confirm**.

### What this assessment does not establish

- **Compute and memory are unquantified on both sides.** The design states no RAM, flash or clock requirement, and measuring the control law's actual execution time is on-target work under a later phase. 480 MHz, 2 MB and 1 MB are recorded as what the board provides, not as what the design needs; no headroom claim is made about them in either direction.
- **The board's usable subset is narrower than the part's, and the allocation has now been checked against it.** The earlier text required the pins a board brings out to be checked against its own schematic before purchase. The header map was recovered from the maker's Altium library and cross-checked with the V12 schematic PDF; the encumbered pins are identified. The QSPI and SPI-flash pairings are noted PARTIAL in the allocation file, with a note to verify against the PDF before committing the allocation. The five unparsed header designators remain.
- **Selection is recorded as chosen, not yet in the graph.** D1 is [chosen] in `decisions.md`; the graph carries no record of it (see the opening note on `DEC-PREHARDWARE-TARGET-FAMILY`).

### Provenance classes

Three, and they differ on more than price. The class is recorded per channel when the part is chosen.

- **Reused** — already carried by this machine, whether it stays where it is or is recovered off a board being replaced. Costs nothing, and has demonstrated it survives this machine's environment, which is more than a datasheet establishes (`REQ-COST-DISCIPLINE-001.C1`). A part coming off the OEM control board is reuse on this definition: it loses its board, not its history.
- **Salvaged** — taken from a different machine. Costs nothing and has demonstrated *neither* a datasheet nor survival here, so a salvaged row records how the part was verified against its specification before it is admitted. What that verification has to reach is set by what the channel's failure costs, not by what the part costs (`REQ-COST-DISCIPLINE-001.C4`).
- **Bought** — new, carrying its datasheet and its price.
