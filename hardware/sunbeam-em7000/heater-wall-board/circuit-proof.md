# Circuit proof: Sunbeam EM7000 heater wall board

The record `docs/board-design-method.md` (section "Proving value-critical circuits before layout") requires before any footprint is placed: one row for every value-critical circuit, with its figure, where the figure comes from, how the circuit is shown to meet it under the worst conditions it will see, and whether it does. This is that record for the heater wall board, for the parts and values of `schematic-spec.yaml`. It follows the form of the main board's `circuit-proof.md`.

## How to read it

- **Met** is `yes` only when the result meets the figure under every condition the row lists. A row is **NO** when its source does not bound a figure the row needs, or when the circuit does not meet the figure; what closes it is written in the Met cell. No figure here was filled in where its source is silent, and no distance or rating was relaxed.
- The arithmetic is `circuit-proof/W_margins.py` with its run `circuit-proof/W_margins.log` (exit status 0; each line is a PASS, FAIL or NOTE). No SPICE deck is committed: every circuit here is a resistive chain or a datasheet limit.
- **Design change of 2026-11-18** (the design opinion of that day, `.stage0/wall-design-opinion.md`, adopted): the triacs are the ST BTA16-800BWRG (C10065, was BTA16-600BRG, C9100) and the opto-triacs the onsemi MOC3083M (C23552, was the Lite-On MOC3083, C10797; **amended 2026-11-19: surface-mount V-option MOC3083SVM, C899868 (first the plain MOC3083SR2M, C111561), hand-fitted (amended 2026-11-23: fitted by the assembler, the owner's decision of that day), pins 4 and 6 swapped so that pin 6 is on OUT_x and pin 4 on the gate chain; the electrical figures below are the same sheet's and unchanged**); the references are the 9xx series. The rows below are re-derived with the new parts' own sheets.
- The onsemi MOC3081M / MOC3082M / MOC3083M sheet (MOC3083M/D Rev. 2, September 2022, `https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/moc3083m-d.pdf`) and the ST DS2114 Rev 11 sheet (May 2018, the BTA16 family, the Internet Archive's copy of `https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/bta16.pdf`) were read on 2026-11-18, their pages cited by their own page numbers (onsemi: safety ratings p.2, absolute maximum ratings p.3, characteristics p.4, curves p.5; ST: summary p.1, Tables 1 to 3 p.2, Tables 4 and 5 p.3). **Amended 2026-11-18 (schematic review finding 3.1, FAULT-1, safety; continues 1.2 and 2.1): why every outcome that leaves a heater on is acceptable, and on what each part of the 2.0 A rule rests.** Graph nodes read for this section (all by `show`, 2026-11-18):

### LED return shorted on the cable (finding 4.1; amended 2026-11-18)

**The circuit.** `+5V_WB` (from the main board's `+5V` through the link `JP761`) reaches this board on `J931` pin 1, runs through the opto-triac's LED (`U911` or `U921`), and returns on `J931` pin 2 or 3 to the main board's `J761`, then through `R763`/`R773` (180 ohm) and the sink `Q761`/`Q771` to ground (main-board sheet 720). A conductor shorted to ground (or to the chassis) between `J931` and `J761` bypasses `R763`, `R773` and the sink: the LED is then in series with the cable's own resistance and the short's resistance only. That total, R, is in no record: the cable's conductor size and length are unknown (main-board `open-items.md`, the heater cable row; round 4 finding 4.1), so the current is stated as a function of R.

**LED current against R.** I = (`+5V_WB` - VF) / R, with `+5V_WB` 4.75 to 5.25 V (main-board `circuit-proof.md`, rails) and VF 1.5 V max at IF 30 mA (onsemi MOC3083M/D Rev. 2 p.4; the sheet states no VF above 30 mA, so the figures above 30 mA are estimates at the stated 1.5 V). The absolute maximum IF is 60 mA (p.3). The LED turns the opto-triac on at IFT 5 mA max (p.4, at 25 degC; 1.5 times that at -40 degC, Fig. 3 p.5, read off the plot).

|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 ohm | 3.3 mA | 3.8 mA | under IFT: the heater does not fire |

Plainly: **below (4.75 - 1.5) / 0.060 = 54 ohm the LED is over its 60 mA absolute maximum on every rail, and between 54 and 62.5 ohm on the high rail**; no part of either board limits it. The sheet's emitter power limit is 120 mW less 1.41 mW/degC above 25 degC (p.3), 56.6 mW at 70 degC air, which at VF 1.5 V is 37.7 mA (an estimate): the LED is over its power rating below about 99 ohm at the high rail at that air.

**What happens, stated as far as the sources reach.** (1) The LED turns the opto-triac on at any R below about 650 ohm, so the heater fires and runs on with no control (the commanded-off stage cannot stop it: the short is on the far side of the sink). (2) Above 54 ohm the LED is inside its absolute maximum and the heater runs on. (3) Below 54 ohm the LED is overstressed; the sheet states no failure mode for an over-current LED, so whether it fails open (the heater then goes off, fails safe) or short (the heater stays on, the current rising to the cable and rail limit) is **not stated and not sourced**. (4) At the limit of R near zero, the short is the +5 V rail's load: the current is `+5V_WB` over the cable resistance, which is in no record.

**What protects the rail.** Nothing on this board or in the path: `JP761` is an isolating link (a 1x2 2.54 mm header with a shunt, rated 3 A, 'not on a protective device's fault path', main-board `circuit-verdicts.md`, row `JP761`), not a fuse, and `+5V_WB` has no fuse or current limit of its own (main-board `circuit-verdicts.md`, net `+5V_WB`: 'a cable short to ground is limited by the rail's own supply'). ### Independence of the heater chain from the controller (INDEP-1; added 2026-11-18)

Round 1 of the schematic review recorded no INDEP-1 row; this is the statement the records lacked. **What is independent.** This board holds no controller, no clock, no firmware and no supply of its own: its only supply is the LED supply `+5V_WB` entering on `J931`, and the opto-triac's output side is galvanically separate from it, VISO 4170 Vrms (onsemi p.4; the exception is W-2). The off state is fail-safe: with the LED undriven (controller in reset or unpowered, cable loose, either conductor open) the leakage is 0.2 mV to 50 mV across the gate network against VGD 0.2 V (row c), so the triac stays off. **What is not independent.** The command to turn on comes from the controller, so a failure to turn off that begins on this board (a shorted triac, a shorted opto-triac output, a shorted varistor, an LED return shorted to ground on the cable) leaves the heater on, and the controller that commands the heater cannot be relied on to turn it off. That outcome rests on the main board's interlock relay `K201`, whose contact is the only feed of `LINE1` to every load. `K201` is independent of the triac chain and of the mechanism that fails it, and its time base, reset and supply take nothing from the controller's clock, reset or supply (main board `circuit-verdicts.md`, Whole board and Interlock rows, INDEP-1 PASS); it is not independent of the controller in the sense of `DEC-CHAIN-FAULT-BOUNDED-BY-INDEPENDENT-ELEMENT`, because it opens when the controller stops refreshing the proof of life, and a controller that keeps refreshing while the heater is on is not bounded by it (main board `open-items.md`, item (i), carried under `DEC-MAIN-BOARD-RESIDUAL-RISKS-ACCEPTED-BY-OWNER`; this record does not claim more). The element's own thermostats and cutoffs are the remaining bound, independent of the controller, and the firmware stuck-on threshold is the controller's own check. The owner has accepted the residual risks that follow (W-1 to W-4). Per `DEC-CHAIN-FAULT-BOUNDED-BY-INDEPENDENT-ELEMENT` (a fault is acceptable when an element independent of the failed part and of the controller still bounds the hazard), the heater-on outcomes are bounded by the element's thermostats and cutoffs, which are independent of both, and, for a stopped controller only, by K201 (what K201 is, what releases its coil and how fast: the amended paragraph above the table); the fuse bounds a short circuit and not a stuck-on heater. The in-service detection of a stuck-on heater rests on `OBL-CONTROLLER-FAULT-RESPONSE-001.C2` and, for the partly failed varistor, on the 2.0 A bound above; the power-up detection rests on `DEL-HW-BRING-UP.C23` and `SOL-HW-MAIN-BOARD-PROVEN-AND-LAID-OUT.C19`. The acceptance of these outcomes for findings 1.2, 2.1 and 3.1 is `owner-sign-off.md` W-6, accepted by the owner, David Baker, in his words "Accept 1 2 and 3", 2026-11-19, including the LED return shorted on the cable.

## The gate-network resistors: the closed set, and each one's rating against its stress

**Definition.** A mains gate-network resistor is a resistor with a pad on a net between an opto-triac output and a triac gate. A reader derives the set from the schematic's own netlist (`kicad-cli sch export netlist`), starting at each triac's gate pin and walking back through resistors only; the resistors walked through, when the walk ends on a pin of the opto-triac, are the set. **Closed set: R911, R912, R921, R922.** The board places no other resistor: the schematic sheets (100, 200, 300 and 400) and the top sheet carry R911, R912, R921 and R922 as the only parts with a reference starting R, and `parts-specification.yaml` lists the same four. - **Peak.** 253 V x sqrt(2) = 357.8 V.
- **False trigger at the line peak.** The pair carries the whole peak: I = 357.8 V / 396 ohm = 0.904 A; each resistor drops 0.904 A x 198 ohm = 178.9 V (the ideal split, 357.8 V / 2) and dissipates 0.904 A x 178.9 V = 0.904 squared x 198 ohm = 162 W. Against the rating: 178.9 / 7.07 = 25.3 times the RCWV, 178.9 / 17.7 = 10.1 times the overload voltage, 162 W / 0.25 W = 647 times the rated power.
