# Owner sign-off list: Sunbeam EM7000 heater wall board

This is the record of the project owner's acceptances for the heater wall board, written 2026-11-18 after schematic review round 2 (`schematic-review.md`, Findings 2.1 to 2.11 and "Round 1 findings, closure") found the owner's decisions of that day entered in no file. It follows the form of the main board's `main-board/owner-sign-off.md`: one entry per decision, each saying who signed, on what day, which finding it closes, and on what ground. Each entry carries `accepted_by` and `accepted_on`, the pair the method's step 5 asks of a safety finding closed by a recorded reason.

**Two kinds of entry, marked in each.** "The owner's own words" means David Baker, the project owner, accepted it himself on 2026-11-18 in the working session, after the risks were put to him in plain terms: no shock risk in normal use; the residuals are fault behaviour inside an enclosure, contained by the interlock relay K201, the fuse, the firmware's stuck-on current threshold, the earth bond measured at bring-up, and an RCD-protected outlet with the lamp current limiter at first power. His words, the only words of his this record quotes: "I'm happy with these and sign as accepted". They cover W-1 to W-4. "Signed on behalf" means the design owner decided it and signed it on the project owner's behalf under his standing instruction of 2026-11-07 (sign owner items on his behalf and list them at the end of the work; stop only for a real safety or performance impact); no word of his is quoted for those entries. They are W-5 and W-6.

**This list does not change the separation basis.** `hardware/sunbeam-em7000/separation-basis.yaml` is a signed record and is not edited here. Its heater-switch barrier names no part, rating or exception for the onsemi opto-triac, the triac tab pair or this board's pad pairs, and its 42 exception entries name no reference of this board. The acceptances W-2 and W-4 are the owner's, but the basis does not yet carry them: the entries to add are listed in `open-items.md`, "What the owner must re-declare in the separation basis before the barrier parts are ordered".

## Look at these first

- W-3: a partly failed varistor fed through the element conducts about 2.7 A (about 340 W in the varistor and element) and no fuse clears it. The firmware's stuck-on current threshold, with every heater commanded off, must trip at 2.0 A or less. That is a requirement for the graph; at this commit no node carries it and no firmware parameter was changed.
- W-2 and W-4: the basis does not carry them yet; the owner re-declares it before the order.
- W-6: signed on your behalf, not in your words: every failure of the heater chain that leaves a heater on is accepted as bounded by independent elements (the element thermostats and cutoffs; K201 only if the controller stops refreshing; the fuse only for a short circuit), with the in-service stuck-on detection resting on a requirement no firmware yet meets. Read it first.
- W-5: three items signed on behalf, one with a new fact for the owner: the triac's current share at its case temperature is short of the steam element's current under either reading of ST's curve.

The ground, `DEC-CHAIN-FAULT-BOUNDED-BY-INDEPENDENT-ELEMENT` (a fault in a part of a chain is acceptable when another element of the chain, independent of the failed part and of the controller, still bounds the hazard): the element's own series thermostats and cutoffs (110 degC coffee, 200 degC steam, which the decision says the reference machine does not establish to be non-resetting, with a non-restoring device owed and not fitted) are the bound that does not depend on the controller; the main board's interlock relay K201 (Hongfa HF115F-I, one contact in the only feed of `LINE1`; its coil is held by FETs that open it at most 100 ms after the controller stops refreshing the proof of life, a figure its record holds only for a coil inductance of 4.73 H or less, `circuit-proof.md`, section on failure modes) bounds only the case of a controller that stops refreshing, not a running controller; the entry fuse F101 (10 A slow-blow) bounds a short circuit and does not clear a stuck-on heater at 4.80 or 5.38 A or the 2.7 A of a partly failed varistor. Detection: at power-up, a threshold set by `DEL-HW-BRING-UP.C23` from the measured all-outputs-off current (the median plus the larger of 5 mA and 6 times the spread, at most 50 mA above the median) against the 50 mA resolution of `SOL-HW-MAIN-BOARD-PROVEN-AND-LAID-OUT.C19`, some 40 times finer than 2.0 A, once the firmware check of `REQ-ACTUATION-CONFIRMATION-001.C6` exists, which no node delivers today; in service, `OBL-CONTROLLER-FAULT-RESPONSE-001.C2` (an actuation occurring without being commanded is detected), a requirement no node delivers at this commit, with 2.0 A as the number any design of it must meet for the partly failed varistor and a persistence that does not read one half cycle of nuisance conduction as stuck-on. `SOL-HW-HEATER-WALL-BOARD-PROVEN-AND-LAID-OUT.C15` is the criterion this entry and W-3 answer. What it leaves: a heater stuck on with a running controller is held only by the element's thermostats until the in-service detection is built; a resetting thermostat cycles the element at its trip.
